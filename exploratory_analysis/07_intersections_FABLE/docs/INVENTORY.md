# Orchestrator inventory: what the five workstreams found, and what made the story

Five analysts (A-E) each wrote a `results/ws_*/findings.md`. This file is the orchestrator's
pass over all 46 findings: which are kept for the HTML story, which are folded into another
finding as a sentence, and which are parked (true but not load-bearing, or too weak to show).
The filter asked three questions of each finding: Is it at the intersection of the two
projects (not already in either)? Would a board member drawing boundaries in November 2026
act differently knowing it? Does it hold up (effect size, n, caveats)?

## A correction to the shared brief

The brief described Glenwood as a closure "candidate". Public record (WUNC, Chapelboro, ABC11)
shows the Board voted unanimously on June 4, 2026 to close Glenwood by fall 2027, chose it over
Ephesus and Seawell because it is a lottery magnet with no attendance boundary (fewer students
displaced, no new busing), moved its Mandarin dual-language track to Rashkis in August 2026, and
on September 17, 2026 voted 4-2 to end its World Language track. The survey opened eleven days
later. Every finding below is read in that light: the closure is decided; what the survey informs
is the district-wide rezoning that follows, with draft scenarios in November 2026 and a vote on
January 21, 2027.

## The narrative arc the kept findings support

1. **Two datasets, one decision.** Module 1 says the decline is two-thirds fewer children and one-third families choosing elsewhere. Module 2's survey asks nothing about the first and little about the second (K1, K2).
2. **Who answered.** The survey is a survey of current parents of current students: the families who left are 14x under-represented, half of the children the boundaries will serve are not yet in school, and the pooled results are the parents' results (K3, K4, K5).
3. **The money.** The board's $38.3M case rests on an enrollment path steeper than demographics alone; the closure study's flat forecast is the demographics-only path; the two differ by about a school (K6, K7, K8, K9).
4. **The boundaries.** The board's top challenge is real and growing, but the closure decision left the three emptiest schools open; filling them is the November task (K10, K11).
5. **What "balance" means.** The widest parent/staff gap; respondents read it as boundaries; the emptiest schools are the poorest; Rashkis receives Glenwood's program (K12-K15, K23).
6. **Distance and walking.** The value the board rates but will not rank; walk flags vs walking; the emptiest schools have the most walkers; the district's own price per converted walker (K16-K20).
7. **Trust.** The loudest school-specific voice is about process; exit talk is rare and tied to programs; the PTA's 41% (K21, K22).
8. **For the board.** Six things to carry into the November scenarios, and what this analysis cannot say.
